# Zulu historical cast: oral testimony, royal houses and succession

**19 October 2026 — research and design only.** Substantial companion to the [18 October evidence brief](zulu_chiefly_coalitions_research_2026_10_18.md). The earlier brief remains a record of that pass; the dated corrections here supersede its narrower availability findings. No runtime files, approval markers or game state were changed.

## Decision

Expand the early cast through identifiable councillors, commanders, royal women and a court poet. Prioritise **Dambuza, Mawa, Nongalaza, Sotobe and Masiphula**, then the more conditional opening placements below. Bring the existing **Mnyamana** into consideration during Mpande's reign. Reuse the existing **Hamu** identity and reconsider its 1879 availability gate: there is concrete political evidence by **1865**, well before the invasion. These are research recommendations, not implementation approval.

The current design uses **allegiance traits**, not forced Royal/Chiefly character ideologies. Keep three separate records: constitutional tendency; support for a named sovereign or claimant; and broad law ideology. A person can favour monarchy while opposing its incumbent. Royal birth, chiefly office and military service do not independently establish any of those three positions.

## Scope and evidence method

This is a targeted cast and succession investigation, not a systematic review or complete genealogy. It uses edited James Stuart testimony hosted by UCT's EMANDULO, scholarly articles and dissertations, a published documentary collection, and a specialist historical essay. Searches followed names, spelling variants, royal houses, offices and dated actions. Access and metadata were checked on 19 October 2026. Printed page numbers are used unless specified otherwise.

Recorded oral testimony is historical evidence. Its assessment needs the speaker, recording date, relationship to events, transmission and editorial mediation; it does not belong in a default “legendary” category. Equally, a colonial memorandum records an observer's or official's account and interests, not unmediated truth. Repetition across scholarship drawing on the same interview is not independent corroboration.

The tables distinguish **attested action**, **availability inference**, and **game interpretation**. “Unknown” constitutional alignment means the research has not established a preference; it does not assert that the person was historically neutral. No exact birthday is newly established here. A source's historical death or succession date does not require that a counterfactual surviving character disappear on schedule.

## Hamu: one person, two genealogical descriptions

**Hamu kaNzibe and Hamu kaMpande refer to the same prince in the evidence consulted.** The editors of Ndabazezwe kaMfuleni's testimony explicitly distinguish Mpande as biological father from Nzibe as genealogical father. This is an editorial identification, not a verbatim statement by Ndabazezwe. [1, p. 194, n. 9](https://emandulo.apc.uct.ac.za/collection/KCAL/4_VOL4/KCAL_JSAVol4_1986_edited_notes_Ndabazezwe_ka_Mfuleni_116_p183_p197.pdf)

Mayinga associates Hamu with Mfemfe, formerly Nzibe's establishment, assigned by Mpande in continuation of that house. This supports house succession; “adoption” alone obscures the distinction. [2, p. 252](https://emandulo.apc.uct.ac.za/collection/KCAL/2_VOL2/KCAL_JSAVol2_1979_edited_notes_Mayinga_ka_Mbekuzana_070_p246_p263.pdf)

Hoye records a statement attributed to Mpande assigning Hamu's paternity to Nzibe and Mbuyazi's to Shaka. The editors caution that both are normally accepted as Mpande's sons; their note places Nzibe's death in 1828, citing Bryant. Do not turn this attributed dynastic statement into established biological paternity for either prince. No separate earlier Hamu was identified in this search; that is a search result, not proof that no namesake ever existed. [3, pp. 167, 173, nn. 5–6](https://emandulo.apc.uct.ac.za/collection/KCAL/1_VOL1/KCAL_JSAVol1_1976_edited_notes_Hoye_ka_Soxalase_028_p167_p173.pdf)

| Window | What the evidence establishes | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|

The scheme does **not** prove that Hamu accepted it. A named royal alternative and territorial notable by the mid-1860s; this is more than an inference from adulthood. |
| January 1878 | Ndabazezwe recalls Hamu ordering uThulwana to obtain spears during its clash with iNgobamakhosi. He spoke from the opposing regiment's experience. [1, pp. 183–184](https://emandulo.apc.uct.ac.za/collection/KCAL/4_VOL4/KCAL_JSAVol4_1986_edited_notes_Ndabazezwe_ka_Mfuleni_116_p183_p197.pdf) | Supports regimental authority and a particular confrontation; does not justify a permanent “reckless” or “cowardly” personality. |
| 1879 | Knight describes Hamu's defection and royal aspiration, while explaining that his assignment to Nzibe's house barred the ordinary succession route. [5, pp. 3–5](https://www.anglozuluwar.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/AZWHS-The-Zulu-Princes-Who-Fought-For-The-British.pdf) | Permit a **contested pretender**, if the design admits alternative royal claims, rather than treating him as the automatic senior legitimate heir. |

**Recommendation:** include the existing Hamu identity in a future claimant-eligibility review, with the distinction between ordinary succession and a politically supported pretender made explicit. His house affiliation should neither erase his historical royal aspirations nor award automatic precedence over Cetshwayo. `ambitious` is a defensible but interpretive option for the later career; no further personality trait is justified. Royal replacement and a later defence of chiefly autonomy are different contexts for allegiance. Neither warrants a forced law ideology. An 1858 report of his support for Cetshwayo surfaced in a dissertation search but could not be fully checked; it is not used to set an earlier gate.

## Concrete event relationships

These are evidence-grounded scene possibilities, not prescribed mechanics or claims that every participant has a known allegiance.

1. **Dingane's council and the Port Natal agreement, 1835–1838.** Dambuza and Ndlela make a named council response possible when handling fugitives and coastal intermediaries. The 1835 agreement concerns claims over people and property and the return of future fugitives; it does not prove enthusiasm for foreign intervention or a free-trade programme. [6, doc. 91](https://boere.co.za/select-constitutional-documents-illustrating-south-african-history-1795-1910/)
2. **Mawa's refugees and royal cattle, 1843 onward.** Hughes links Mawa's flight, Mpande's demand for cattle's return, confiscation by **Kofiyana kaMbengana kaGwayi**, and Qadi recovery through redistributed cattle. Kofiyana is a useful African coastal intermediary; he need not be converted into a Zululand court chief. **Madlukana**, the Qadi regent while Mqhawe was young, supplies another historically named continuity actor. Do not force the 1843 flight into a campaign where its causes never occur. [8, pp. 52–54](https://emandulo.apc.uct.ac.za/collection/Cele/publications/Politics_and_society_in_Inanda_Natal_the%20Qadi_chief_under_Mqhawe_%28HHughesPhD%29.pdf)
3. **A royal household opens its armoury, 1856.** Langazana's establishment allows a dispute about royal instructions and access to war-shields. Mpambukelwa's narrative includes reported information and admits he did not observe the main battle; it is not an eyewitness account of every action. [11, pp. 292–293](https://emandulo.apc.uct.ac.za/collection/KCAL/3_VOL3/KCAL_JSAVol3_1982_edited_notes_Mpambukelwa_ka_Cangasa_098_p291_p295.pdf)
4. **A minister backs the rival heir.** Masiphula is a concrete way to express divergence between service to the incumbent's government and support for a particular successor. The scene should refer to the attested settlement dispute, not invent an abstract constitutional manifesto.
5. **Praise at a politically difficult gathering.** Magolwana offers a historical voice for remembrance, criticism and legitimacy. Silva's analysis treats praise as politically active performance; criticism within it need not be a repudiation of kingship. Use new prose informed by that role, not an invented poem presented as a recovered historical quotation. [16](https://doi.org/10.1590/0104-87752025v41e25007)
6. **Hamu as an alternative in external diplomacy, 1865 onward.** A foreign attempt to recruit a royal alternative can be presented as an offer with uncertain reception. It must not hard-code Hamu's acceptance merely because a foreign government conceived the plan. The historical basis is the dated scheme above, not his later 1879 conduct.

## Succession when the ruler dies without a designated heir

The evidence supports an **evidence-bounded pool and a contested recognition process**. It does not supply a universal deterministic algorithm or numerical probabilities.

- **Separate eligibility, recognition and effective power.** Hamu illustrates why a biologically close royal can have a different house entitlement yet become a political alternative. A candidate list should distinguish an ordinary heir from a pretender; neither category means a guaranteed victory.
- **Separate the king, royal house and claimant coalition.** Mawa can support a royal alternative; Mnyamana can hold royal office; a maternal household can assist a prince. They need not all endorse the current king or all count as pretenders.
- **Do not collapse all royal relatives into one permanent political following.** Kinship supplies relationships, not proof of a single indivisible client base. Conversely, adding every prince as a full new following would exaggerate support. Model a demonstrated distinct house/network only when there is evidence; retain a single representative where the same followers are being represented twice.
- **A regent is not automatically the heir.** The Emgazini case and later Onkweni disputes permit stewardship, competing adult influence and an underage successor. A ruler's death should not require inventing a named child to fill an unknown genealogy.
- **An eldest-son rule is insufficient.** Mandlakazi demonstrates house seniority differing from total birth order. This is a warning about over-simple inheritance, not proof that every royal or chiefly succession followed an identical house rule.
- **Avoid early summons.** An 1836 vacancy may use living and suitably established opening royals and brokers; it cannot summon late-century claimants, ministers or chiefs to reach a target roster size. If evidence runs out, label any designed fallback as counterfactual or fictional rather than historical.
- **Preserve causal alternatives.** A historically defeated prince surviving in a different campaign can retain his own identity and relationships. Historical deaths, exile and colonial appointments are evidence for conditions and available actors, not compulsory future outcomes.

These recommendations are compatible with allegiance traits and existing AF characters. They do not require rewriting characters' broad law ideologies, adding a new UI system, or assigning historical percentages to court support.

## Provenance wording and identity corrections

Prefer **“Historical figure”** as the general status, with an optional short source note such as **“Recorded in oral testimony”** or **“Remembered in praise traditions.”** “Recorded in oral tradition” is acceptable if the detail explains who recorded what and when. It should not be a lower-confidence badge attached to every African figure whose evidence happens to be oral. A contested relationship can be uncertain even when the person's existence is secure.

| Category | Appropriate meaning | Example / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded oral testimony | Identified speaker and recording, distinguishing recollection, inherited account and editorial note. | Hamu's house identity: a concise source note can distinguish biological descent from dynastic affiliation. |
| Praise tradition | A poem/performance transmitted or recorded with its context. | Magolwana's career and his son's remembered performances; poetic imagery is not a literal feat or psychological diagnosis. |
| Reported tradition | An attributed account transmitted through another source. | A disputed royal statement may be reported as such without making its genealogy settled fact. |
| Contested genealogy | A particular relationship or house assignment requires qualification. | Klwana's patronymic; the existing Ntuli relationship dispute. Do not label the whole person doubtful. |
| Counterfactual career | An attested person takes a different course in the campaign. | A surviving Mbuyazi is historical; the later reign authored for him is counterfactual. |
