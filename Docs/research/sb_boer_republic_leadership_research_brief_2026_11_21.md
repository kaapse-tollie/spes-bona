# Who led the early Boer republics?

**Spes Bona research brief · 21 November 2026 · Great Trek to the early 1860s**

## Executive summary

**Hendrik Potgieter founded Potchefstroom.** He proclaimed the settlement on the Mooi River in December 1838, with occupation probably beginning in January 1839 [1, p. 456]. When he moved east to Ohrigstad in 1845, families stayed at Potchefstroom under a local magistrate [1, p. 458]. **Andries Pretorius** stayed in Natal when many other trekkers left in 1843 [2, para. 12]. In 1848 he settled in the Magaliesberg and built support in the western Transvaal [1, p. 453], [3, p. 130]. His son **Marthinus Wessel Pretorius** succeeded him in the western command in 1853 [3, pp. 141–142] and became the South African Republic’s first state president in January 1857 [3, pp. 155–156].

**Give early Potchefstroom to Hendrik, then let him go north when Zoutpansberg forms.** SB currently assigns Potchefstroom to one of the two Pretoriuses [4, Founding effects, lines 575–613], gives Zoutpansberg to Stephanus Schoeman [4, Founding effects, lines 701–714, 769–797], and can choose Hendrik over Cornelis Potgieter for Lydenburg [4, Founding effects, lines 868–876, 983–1013]. Schoeman belongs later in the northern succession, in 1855 [5, p. 27]. Cornelis had chaired the eastern council and spoke for Lydenburg in negotiations [6, pp. 89–90]. My recommendation is to use Hendrik for early TRN and ZPB, Andries for Boer Natalia and then western TRN, and Cornelis for LYD’s constitutional break.

## Introduction

This brief asks who led the people represented by six SB tags. It follows ORA from the inland trekkers through Winburg to the Free State, TRN through Potchefstroom and the western Transvaal, NAL through Boer Natalia and British Natal, and the regional communities of KLR (Klip River), ZPB (Zoutpansberg) and LYD (Lydenburg). The account ends with Marthinus Wessel Pretorius’s Free State presidency.

Several offices matter to the mapping. Maritz and Potgieter brought distinct followings to the first combined camp [3, pp. 118–120]. When the two men disagreed, trekkers chose which leader to follow [3, p. 122]. In Natalia the *Volksraad* was the elected council, while *landdrosts* served as local magistrates [3, pp. 123–125]. In the Transvaal the Volksraad could appoint a different commandant-general for each region [3, p. 135]. The later republican constitution created a presidency with executive authority [3, p. 156]. These titles describe offices within the settler communities. Mpande also pursued his own objectives when dealing with the trekkers [2, para. 9].

## Research approach

On 21 November 2026 I compared English- and Afrikaans-language scholarship on SciELO South Africa with university journals and the Pretoria and UNISA repositories. Searches included *Soutpansberg/Zoutpansberg*, *landdros*, *voorsitter* and *kommandant-generaal*. I checked the delegates’ titles in the government-printed Sand River Convention [7, p. 2, list of delegates]. I compared the constitutional historian’s account of the four commands [3, p. 135] with the office register’s citation to the Volksraad resolution of 15 January 1851 [8, n. 2].

For the SB comparison I followed country history, founding effects, the Blood River handoff, the British takeover, both later departure routes and the younger Pretorius’s eligibility. Script citations identify the linked file by its label in the references and give line numbers in the inspected working tree. Page numbers refer to printed pages. For Liebenberg’s unpaginated biography, the counting convention is given with the reference.

### Boer Natalia, British Natal and Klip River, 1838–48

**Who governed Boer Natalia?** Its elected **Volksraad** chose a chairman for each sitting [3, pp. 123–124]. It met regularly at Pietermaritzburg from 1839, with **J. J. Burger** as secretary [3, p. 125]. **Andries Pretorius** held senior military command and many followers looked to him for political leadership [3, p. 125]. A game portrait of Andries can represent that influence. The Volksraad accepted British authority in August 1843 [13, p. 25]. Natal was formally annexed as a British colony in August 1845 [3, p. 126], and the colonial administration was in place by December [13, p. 24].

**Who led the Klip River affair?** **Andries Theodorus Spies** had served as commandant and magistrate at Weenen [11, p. 172]. He took part in the first Boer delegation to **King Mpande** in February 1847 [13, p. 28] and spoke for the seceding Klip River settlers in June [13, p. 37]. **J. H. de Lange** received the king’s envoys and brought the settlers together to discuss the boundary [13, pp. 28–29]. Lieutenant-Governor **Martin West** appointed **J. N. Boshof** resident magistrate for the district [13, p. 35]. Leśniewski places the collapse of the Klip River project in late 1847 and treats the appearance of Andries **Pretorius** as the start of a subsequent phase [13, pp. 36–37]. He interprets Mpande’s dealings with the settlers as part of an independent Zulu foreign policy [14, Abstract].

**Where did Andries Pretorius go?** After Blood River he settled on a farm near Pietermaritzburg [2, para. 8]. When many Boers left Natal in 1843, he chose to stay [2, para. 12]. He later travelled to Grahamstown to petition the Cape governor, Sir Henry Pottinger, who declined to see him [2, para. 12]. In 1848 he left Natal [1, p. 453] and settled in the **Magaliesberg**, west of present-day Tshwane [3, p. 130]. His farm, **Grootplaats**, was near today’s Hartbeespoort Dam [2, para. 16]. The Winburg Boers invited him south to lead their resistance in June 1848 [2, para. 14]. British forces defeated him at **Boomplaats** on 29 August [2, para. 14]. He continued to speak for Potchefstroom interests north of the Vaal [1, p. 453], [2, para. 15]. **For SB, I would make TRN his home.** The invitation from Winburg explains the connection to ORA [2, para. 14].

### The regional commands of 1851

On **15 January 1851** the Volksraad authorised four regional commandants-general [8, n. 2]. A year later, the Sand River Convention recognised self-government for the emigrant farmers north of the Vaal [7, p. 2, first clause]. Its delegate list identifies both Pretorius and Joubert as commandants-general [7, p. 2, list of delegates].

### Who succeeded them?

- **Zoutpansberg.** At Ohrigstad, Hendrik had disputed the Volksraad’s authority with the faction associated with **J. J. Burger** [3, pp. 128–131]. Field cornet **Jan Valentyn Botha** led the party that set out from Ohrigstad in April 1848 while Hendrik was at Potchefstroom [5, p. 25]. Hendrik subsequently joined the settlement [5, p. 25] and led its development [5, Summary, para. 1]. He held its regional command when the Volksraad made the four appointments in 1851 [8, “Zoutpansberg” and n. 2]. After Hendrik died in December 1852, his son **Pieter Johannes (“Piet”) Potgieter** took the northern command in March 1853 [5, p. 27], [8, “Zoutpansberg”]. Piet died during the conflict with **Mokopane** in 1854 [5, p. 27]. **Stephanus Schoeman** succeeded to the command in 1855 [8, “Zoutpansberg”] and that year replanned and renamed the town later known as Schoemansdal [5, Summary, para. 1].
- **Lydenburg.** The decision to move from Ohrigstad came in 1849, and the new town took shape early in 1850 [6, p. 88]. **Cornelis Potgieter** had been Ohrigstad’s first landdrost and had chaired several council meetings [6, p. 89]. He spoke for Lydenburg alongside **Jacob de Clerq** and **H. T. Bührmann** [6, pp. 89–90]. **W. F. Joubert** held the district’s military command [8, “Lydenburg”]. After consulting the district’s inhabitants, Lydenburg’s representatives voted to separate on **16 December 1856** [15, p. 138]. Its full Volksraad ratified the decision on **11 March 1857** [15, p. 138]. Reunification with the South African Republic followed in 1860 [6, p. 90]. Hendrik had died in 1852 [5, p. 27].
- **Western Transvaal and the South African Republic.** The elder **Andries Pretorius** negotiated the **Sand River Convention of 17 January 1852** [3, p. 138] and died in July 1853 [2, para. 16]. His son **Marthinus Wessel Pretorius** succeeded him as western commandant-general that August [3, pp. 141–142], [8, “Potchefstroom-Rustenburg”]. Marthinus took office as the republic’s first state president in **January 1857** [3, pp. 155–156]. Lydenburg rejected the constitutional settlement, and Schoeman in the north refused the military office the new government offered him [3, pp. 155–156].
- **Winburg and the Orange Free State.** British rule over the **Orange River Sovereignty** ran from 1848 to 1854 [3, pp. 130, 142]. After British withdrawal **Josias Philippus Hoffman** became the Free State’s first president in 1854 [16, “Britain’s withdrawal from Bloemfontein”], [17, Hoffman entries]. **Jacobus Nicolaas Boshof**, the former Klip River magistrate [13, p. 35], held the presidency in 1855–59 [17, Boshof entry]. **Marthinus Wessel Pretorius** held it in **1860–63** [17, Marthinus Wessel Pretorius entry]. The son therefore supplies the presidential connection between ORA and TRN. His father’s southern connection was the call from Winburg in 1848 [2, para. 14].

### Event-writing evidence check, 22 November 2026

Andries stayed in Natal when many families left in 1843 [2, para. 12]. By 1848 he had moved to the Magaliesberg [3, p. 130]. Marthinus convened a meeting at Potchefstroom in December 1856 and served on the committee appointed to revise the laws [3, pp. 154–155, sec. 2.3.3.3]. These passages support the western setting and the son’s connection to constitutional politics. My interpretation for the events is to show the farming constituency choosing a spokesman and leave the presidency to an election. The fictional petition, the Youth trait, age 35 and the four-year popularity award are authorial choices. The accounts cited here supply no historical magnitude for them.

For this targeted addition I rechecked Liebenberg’s signed biography at Encyclopaedia Africana and Van der Merwe’s paginated article through SciELO and the journal’s DOI record on 22 November 2026. The biography supplies the order of Andries’s movements. The constitutional study identifies the meeting and committee in its account of the drafts.

## Limitations and evidence gaps

A dated appointment can be checked more precisely than a shift in allegiance. The register traces the four commands to a specific Volksraad resolution [8, n. 2], and the independent Sand River delegate list confirms the titles actually used by Pretorius and Joubert [7, p. 2]. For changes in popular support, the evidence is of a different kind. Van den Bergh identifies local commandant Gert Kruger among the Potgieter supporters who turned towards Pretorius [1, p. 461]. I treat that as evidence of a gradual shift. I also treat agreement between later accounts that cite the same minutes as a single line of evidence [3, p. 135, n. 61], [8, n. 2].

Jooste dates Lydenburg’s republican beginnings to **1854** and identifies Cornelis’s part in the negotiations [6, p. 90]. Vogel distinguishes the **1856 decision** to separate from its **1857 ratification** [15, p. 138]. SB’s event text explicitly dates its resolution to **11 March 1857** [4, Event descriptions, lines 34–36]. That makes the ratification the closer match for this event.

For Klip River I used Leśniewski’s full chronological reconstruction for the settlers’ actions and the end of the secession [13, pp. 28–30, 35–37]. For his later interpretation of Mpande’s policy I could verify the publisher’s abstract [14, Abstract]. Both works are by the same historian [13, p. 23, byline], [14, byline]. For Mocke I used Theal’s digitised index entry, which identifies the party and the confrontation [12, index, s.v. “Mocke, Commandant Jan”]. Andries’s documented move west followed the British takeover [2, para. 12], [3, p. 130]. Sending him to the same destination after a punitive Zulu victory is a design inference.

## Recommendations

1. **Give Hendrik the founding of Potchefstroom and the move to Zoutpansberg.** Give ORA a local successor before transferring its living founder to TRN. When ZPB forms, move Hendrik north if available. Use Schoeman as the fallback or later successor. This compresses Piet Potgieter’s historically brief command [8, “Zoutpansberg”]. Protect Hendrik’s general role in TRN’s formation and cleanup, which currently favour other templates [4, Founding effects, lines 328–340, 623–661]. Omit Tregardt from Potchefstroom’s starting cast. Winburg already had local magistrates historically [11, p. 176], and Mocke had a following of his own [12, index, s.v. “Mocke, Commandant Jan”]. Ordinary succession can represent that local authority without another named character.
2. **Send Andries to TRN when he actually leaves Natal.** Keep his Blood River role and the player’s choice of country. Update the TRN prompt, which assumes his son takes over [19, Handoff and arrival text, lines 58–62]. On the British path, let West take office while Andries stays until departure. At either departure, send the living Andries to TRN if viable and use the existing fallback otherwise. If Hendrik has gone north and the western office is open, favour Andries. If Hendrik is still at Potchefstroom, let Andries serve as commander until Hendrik leaves. Respect a different elected incumbent. Preserve each route’s arrival notification [20, Colonial and population effects, lines 518–548], [20, County and departure effects, lines 866–869]. Keep the existing household shares and the rule that 95% of each Boer pop, rounded down, departs when a destination is available [20, Colonial and population effects, lines 714–726, 729–789]. An outcome that preserves Boer Natalia can keep its local leadership.
3. **Use Cornelis and Marthinus at their respective constitutional moments.** Give Cornelis the LYD secession when available. Give an eligible adult Marthinus a route to the western office at the next succession after his father, or through TRN’s constitutional choice, which already enacts a presidency [4, Republic events, lines 245–259]. This would keep the later union offer and Free State candidacy reachable. Retain Spies in KLR and let ordinary succession handle intervening Winburg officials and most other regional offices.

For a future implementation, check TRN formation on either side of Blood River, Andries arriving before or after Hendrik goes north, and standard and punitive departures with TRN present or absent. Check that a living, eligible Marthinus can reach the later presidency. Preserve character identity through transfers and allow an existing save whose leader has died or already moved to keep its own history. These changes can use the existing tags, journal entries and succession machinery.
