# Land reform in Zululand, Swaziland and Gaza

Design discussion · Victoria 3 1.15.2 · 21 November 2026

**Tenant Farmers is the closest vanilla starting law for ZUL, SWZ and GZA in 1836.** I would change Gaza’s inherited Serfdom too, with the lowest confidence of the three in any single countrywide law. The question is how an ordinary household obtained and kept a field, and what authority that gave its chief. A ruler’s right to summon a regiment, demand tribute or hold captives answers a different question. All three starts currently inherit Serfdom and have an explicit Amabutho army law. [Supplied historical brief](research/sb_zul_swz_gza_land_tenure_research_brief_2026_11_21.md) · [Zulu start](<../common/history/countries/zul - zulu.txt>) · [Swazi start](<../common/history/countries/swz - swaziland.txt>) · [Gaza start](<../common/history/countries/gza - gaza.txt>)

## Who could claim the field or the harvest?

### ZUL · Dingane

Guy describes two decisions at different levels. Political allegiance gave a homestead access to land. Within it the head assigned plots, milk cows and grain storage to each wife and her children. Women did most of the cultivation, and each house had a claim on what it produced. This gave people in the same homestead recognisable interests of their own even while they depended on chiefly and royal authority. Guy reconstructs chiefly the 1870s. For Dingane’s reign, the archaeology of his capital at uMgungundlovu gives firmer evidence of a separate royal and regimental household. Calling men into royal service is a good fit for SB’s Amabutho System. Extending that obligation to every family’s field as a landlord’s labour due would require further evidence. [Guy, pp. 33–36, 40–43](https://emandulo.apc.uct.ac.za/collection/Jeff_Guy/K/Guy_1981_Production_Zulu_kingdom.pdf) · [Van der Merwe and Pikirayi on the royal homesteads](https://doi.org/10.1080/0067270X.2018.1540218) · [SB Amabutho System](../common/laws/03_sb_amabutho_system.txt)

Dingane’s 1835 agreement with the British residents at Port Natal also shows where his authority over movement had to be negotiated. Earlier fugitives received a pardon and remained liable to summons. The residents undertook to refuse or return future fugitives. The agreement concerned people crossing a particular frontier. It gives little basis for extending Tenant Farmers’ ban on internal peasant migration across Zululand. [Port Natal treaty, arts. 1–3, with source notes](research/sb_zul_swz_gza_land_tenure_research_brief_2026_11_21.md#zul--household-production-royal-service-and-restricted-refuge)

### SWZ · Sobhuza I

Sobhuza incorporated chiefdoms of different origins, and a royal appointment could precede effective occupation by years. Bonner finds a stronger chiefly presence in the south and a more lightly occupied conquest zone farther north. He dates the wider network of royal villages and the more systematic mobilisation of young men to cultivate royal fields to Mswati’s reign. Starting with Serfdom would project that later concentration of power back onto Sobhuza’s kingdom and across communities where royal authority was still uneven. [Bonner on the conquest state](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/kings-commoners-and-concessionaires/conquest-state-18201838/FD32B1AB3EB373E6811AB5B8861D98E7) · [Bonner on the change under Mswati, pp. 8–10](https://sas-space.sas.ac.uk/4059/1/P_L_Bonner_-_Classes%2C_the_mode_of_production_and_the_state_in_pre-colonial_Swaziland.pdf)

Hughes later recorded an allocation of arable land or a house site to someone acting for a family. This helps explain how chiefly political power could coexist with a family’s claim to cultivate. The procedure was recorded in the twentieth century. The stronger inference for 1836 is that households relied on local authority and that Sobhuza’s ability to intervene varied across his kingdom. [Hughes on Swazi land tenure, p. 258 n. 2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/africa/article/abs/some-swazi-views-on-land-tenure1/9690A8F8817465AA5583D1E3BC226A07)

### GZA · Soshangane

In 1833–34 António Nobre reported the detention of messengers and negotiations with Soshangane over payments in beads. That establishes power over travel on a particular route. It leaves open who could give a family a field in the surrounding settlements. Liesegang’s reconstruction from 1840 onward makes the distinction concrete. Captives could be incorporated into dominant households near the centre, while subject chiefs could retain their own organisation and answer to a Nguni house for tribute. Mathebula also finds different relationships among neighbouring Hlengwe groups. The evidence for household plots is thinnest here, particularly for assigning a single practice to either of SB’s two Gaza holdings in January 1836. [Santos on Nobre’s account, pp. 78–80](https://doi.org/10.11606/T.8.2017.tde-05102017-183101) · [Liesegang, pp. 178–184](https://ir.uz.ac.zw/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10646/3686/Liesegang_Notes_on_the_internal_structure_of_The_Gaza_Kingdom.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) · [Mathebula on Gaza and the Hlengwe](https://newcontree.org.za/index.php/nc/article/view/247/986)

This is why I would reverse the earlier recommendation to retain Serfdom in Gaza. It makes the coercion experienced by some households the rule for every cultivator. Peasant Proprietorship goes too far in the other direction by assigning independent ownership. Tenant Farmers leaves chiefly authority politically consequential. SB already gives the player a more precise choice about tribute in *The Collector’s Hand*, when Soshangane can dispatch royal collectors or work through the existing chiefs. Both choices make sense with the same national land law. [Gaza event](../events/sb_gaza_events.txt) · [Event text](../localization/english/sb_eastern_sphere_l_english.yml)

## What the two designs let the player change

| Design | What the player starts with | What a reform means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Use vanilla Tenant Farmers** | The Landowners gain **+25% political strength** from land. This represents chiefs’ leverage over access to fields even if the army model later changes. | Enacting Homesteading in ZUL or SWZ, or Peasant Proprietorship in GZA, shifts political weight towards farmers and gives them an ownership stake. The starting tooltip already describes land as the property of hereditary elites, so granting estate titles cannot itself be a distinct transition. |
| **Recognise Customary Land Tenure as one shared law** | A household holds recognised rights to cultivate through its community. The chief’s role in allocating or upholding those rights earns the same **+25% Landowners strength**. ZUL and SWZ are the clearest cases. In GZA the relevant authority could be a tributary chief. | Enacting Tenant Farmers would turn chiefly authority into hereditary estate ownership. A proposed **Agrarian approval** of customary tenure would give Rural Folk reason to resist that change. **Hierarchic approval** of both laws would allow chiefs to accept either settlement. A reform towards smallholder ownership would give farmers an independent claim and weaken chiefly leverage. |

In vanilla the **Hierarchic** ideology approves of Tenant Farmers and the **Agrarian** ideology disapproves. The custom design earns its place if turning household use rights into hereditary estates should be a political choice. Under that design I would also drop the land law’s blanket ban on peasants moving between states. A family settling elsewhere could still depend on acceptance by the local chief. The example mods make different assumptions about who controls land. Hail Columbia’s Land Stewardship inherits Collectivized Agriculture and expands worker ownership. Gates of the Bosphorus’s Çiftlik is an amendment to Serfdom that empowers officers. Vanilla Manorialism requires tenants to work unpaid for estate owners. Each would add a specific claim about property or labour that the evidence for these starts cannot sustain across an entire kingdom. ## Decision record

| Status | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Agreed | Historical fit in 1836 guides these three starts. |
| Provisional | Use vanilla Tenant Farmers for ZUL, SWZ and GZA. Household cultivation and dependence on chiefly authority make the shared approximation defensible. Gaza’s range of local arrangements makes it the least secure fit. |
| Deferred | Revisit a shared customary law if politically distinguishing household use rights from hereditary estates becomes a design goal. |

None of the March cases was severe. Across all five later checkpoints, the three tags had zero residents below the starvation threshold in the territory each then held. These observations establish an earlier Gaza food crisis and provide healthy comparisons for the other two starts. The intervening years and a fresh January 1836 opening remain unsampled in this campaign.

The February 1853 subsistence farm had **6,630 workers across 70,000 positions**. The maize farm employed **300 of 5,000**, while the ranch employed **4,904 of 5,000**. Tenant Farmers makes it possible for a qualified household member to take a maize job even at that low subsistence staffing. Switching at fixed staffing would initially remove about **0.80 grain**. At the saved maize productivity, **about 175 additional maize workers** would replace it if all came from ordinary subsistence farming. This estimate deducts the base subsistence grain those workers would cease to produce and holds throughput constant. Wages, meat prices and who actually hires decide the food outcome. In March 1843 mainland Zululand had a producing banana plantation and ranch. ZUL's part of Natal had only its subsistence farm and manor house. At the saved staffing, changing to Tenant Farmers would reduce that holding's marketed grain from **0.85 to 0.57** before workers or prices respond. A past claimant-war replay found that Klip River could lose access to mainland food when the markets split. [Claimant-war food evidence](compatibility/zulu_muster_famine_2026_10_29.md)

**The March crisis was concentrated among labourers.** In Lourenço Marques, **168,922** of the 171,009 starving residents belonged to labourer Pops with no recorded workplace. Those households had **42,231 people in the workforce**. The subsistence farms and the maize farm were fully staffed. In Zambezia **all 15,767** starving residents belonged to labourer households employed at the tea plantation. Every peasant household in both holdings was above the starvation threshold. The extra £1 goes to working peasants, and Tenant Farmers' internal migration rule also applies to peasants. The immediate tests for the hungry labourers are paying vacancies and the price of their food. [March save, GZA states 412 and 420](<../../../save games/natalia_1843_03_10.v3>)

At the same March staffing and throughput, the 27 subsistence farm levels in Lourenço Marques give **48.6 grain under Serfdom, 32.4 under Tenant Farmers and 40.5 under Peasant Proprietorship**. The last option converts 1,350 existing peasant positions into farmer positions. In Zambezia both alternatives would remove about **1.28 fruit** from orchard output. Peasant Proprietorship would also add about **0.97 grain**. These are immediate supply calculations. A family without work still needs a paying vacancy or cheaper food, and tea workers may need higher wages or cheaper staples. The five later saves show recovery under the existing law without establishing its cause.
