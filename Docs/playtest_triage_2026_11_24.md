## Law setup and colonial government

**1. Internal Colonisation and the Cape Imperial Establishment — keep the starting setup.** In 1.15.2 the Colonialist ideology approves Internal Colonisation and Colonial Exploitation. It is neutral on Frontier Colonisation, Resettlement and No Colonial Affairs. Internal Colonisation needs the Colonization technology and either colonial country status or an unincorporated state. CAP's Armed Forces get Colonialist and SB's Imperialist ideology, which has no additional colonisation stance. CAP's Frontier Farmers expressly favour Frontier Colonisation. The Cape history leaves it to politics, and CAP did enact Frontier on **1 June 1845** in this run. I would give the Imperial Establishment its own Frontier preference only if a military sponsor is an explicit design goal. The leader and the IG's other ideologies also matter to a particular law vote. **2. Military Bureaucrats — keep Imperial Administration for CAP and British Natal, and Elected Bureaucrats for the Boer republics.** The two imperial administrations already have a hereditary-bureaucracy variant with −25% population bureaucracy cost, +10% aristocrat political strength, attraction of aristocrats to the Armed Forces, and lower liberty desire. Vanilla Military Bureaucrats gives the same cost reduction and +25% political strength to both officers and bureaucrats. That would change whose power the law represents. ORA, TRN, Boer Natalia, ZPB, LYD and KLR have explicit elected-bureaucrat setups. Reserve the military law for an actual military administration if one is authored. The existing Responsible Government handoff changes governance, subjecthood and the extraction economy where applicable. It does not itself appoint a new civil service. In the checkpoint CAP and British Natal later enacted Appointed Bureaucrats in 1874 and 1877 respectively. **3. Responsible Government — confirmed bypass, with an additional recovery problem.** CAP was a Crown Colony in 1862 and activated Theocracy on **27 January 1868**. In October 1885 it was still Britain's colonial subject under a pact dated 1 January 1836. Vanilla already restricts ordinary Governance Principles changes for a subject of type Colony. The save identifies the result and date but does not identify the route that changed the law. A second route exists in the scripts: the subject and overlord can each use a generic Increase Autonomy action, and the Colony type lists Dominion as its higher alternative. Both of SB's Responsible Government actions require the colony currently to have Colonial Administration. The theocratic Cape has therefore lost access to the intended remedy. **6. BST raids — fix selection and the transfer together.** Event `sb_bst_frontier.010` picks a qualifying Vrystaat state and then its largest free pop of at least 100. A Boer pop qualifies today. The second issue is broader: after moving a slice to BUG's capital, the script converts every free pop of the captured culture there and then sends every enslaved pop of that culture to BST. **8. Hamu — a sliding payment is feasible.** The upfront grant already scales, using **100% of the sponsor's gold-reserve capacity** when the offer opens. **9. Swaziland after cession — highest food priority.** In May 1862 state 1158 had 16 arable land, a maize farm, a livestock ranch and mean food security of 0.88. **10. Cape colonisation of Bechuanaland — investigate the Tswana side.** The Cape colony was growing between June and October. In October **95.6%** of people in Tswana Bechuanaland were below the starvation threshold, versus **0.85%** on the Cape side. The Tswana fragment already had two gold-field levels, with a building established in 1872. The Cape fragment had one, established in 1885. In May 1862 the Tswana state had 49,500 residents, 10,716 peasant workers and mean food security of 0.74. In October 1885 it had 2,947 peasant workers and three levels of subsistence farms. **11. Slavery after signing with Britain — enforcement already exists, but the signatory gets no dedicated breach event.** Sand River and Bloemfontein acceptance grant recognition, ban slavery and create a five-year British truce. If an independent ORA or TRN signatory later activates a covered slavery law, including Discrete Inboekstelsel, the law callback queues a hidden British response after **90, 180, 270 or 360 days**. **13. Delagoa Bay — the saves identify a change of hub.** In May 1862 western TRN's active world-market hub was Natal (Durban). Britain granted TRN transit rights and Portugal did not. In October 1885 all three TRN states used **NRP's Zululand** as their active hub. TRN held transit rights from Portugal, Britain and NRP. Natal itself used the Cape hub, and Portuguese Lourenço Marques used Zambezia. Both Durban and Lourenço Marques had level-two ports. These records identify the trade hubs. The saves do not identify every physical port or sea node traversed on a displayed route. [1862 states and transit articles](../../../save%20games/transvaal%20republic_1862_05_01.v3) · [1885 states, world-market hubs and transit articles](../../../save%20games/autosave.v3)

Vanilla selects the first coastal hub in a market area by score. A port level adds **10**, £100,000 of state GDP adds **1**, one unit of trade capacity adds **0.1**, and market-capital status adds **100**. An eligible challenger must **exceed twice** the incumbent's score to displace it. An area can have up to four sufficiently separated hubs. States normally use the nearest eligible hub, with distance and access affecting fallback. For TRN to use Lourenço Marques as a hub, it first has to qualify in the supplying market area. TRN then needs access to that hub. The directed treaty grants access to the source country's world-market hubs without naming one. [World-market defines](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/defines/00_defines.txt>) · [Hub and access concepts](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/localization/english/concepts_l_english.yml>) · [Transit Rights text](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/localization/english/diplomacy_l_english.yml>)

SB requires active railways in western, eastern and northern Transvaal plus qualifying transit access. It gives +33% import advantage on the actor's Transvaal states and +0.15 market access price impact at Lourenço Marques. Neither modifier enters the native hub-score formula directly. Eastern TRN's railway was level zero in October and the reward modifiers were absent. This save therefore cannot measure the completed route's reward. For a future balance test, first complete the eastern railway and record the selected hub, port levels, GDP, trade capacity and displayed path. Then compare a one-level port improvement at Lourenço Marques under the same treaties. A switch is possible only if the port qualifies and wins the relevant comparison. If the aim is commercial benefit regardless of the port chosen, the existing import bonus already serves that purpose. ## Quick patch and checkpoint replay

Items 3, 4 and 6 have gameplay changes. The [working TODO register](../../docs/current_todos.md#deferred-from-the-24-november-2026-playtest) tracks the other thirteen items. Existing campaigns pick up the changes at their next qualifying law change, civil-war split or raid.

For the decisive Zulu replay, begin before a new tracked split. Save immediately before settlement and again after reunion while paused on the same game date. Record the original ZUL country ID before the split. In both saves look up that ID and Natal's ID. Compare both directed truce records and the displayed expiry, treaty IDs and articles, bilateral relations, and overlord and subject IDs. Repeat with a truce that expires or is ended during the war. The postwar comparison should reflect that change.

Run the dynastic and royal–chiefly controllers through both loyalist and rebel victories. Include a backdown, military defeat, foreign leadership and a reload with the demands pending. Check the rebel side's *Take Power in Zululand*, the kingdom's *Annex* demand, war closure, the winning ruler or constitution and each applicable victory notice once. For the Thirteen Chiefdoms and Hamu, check the reserved outcome after the territories reunite. [Reunion regression](../tests/test_zulu_reunion.py)

For the Cape, British Natal and a non-British colony, try a forced government-law change and check the following day, then grant Responsible Government and repeat. Check both autonomy interactions, the autonomy play and war goal, and outright independence alongside a chartered company and a responsible colony. For the frontier raid, compare the victim's treasury and both states' populations immediately around the victim's event option. BST's own 25 raid casualties occur separately when the raid is launched. [Colony and raid regressions](../tests/test_colonial_government_and_bst_raids.py)
