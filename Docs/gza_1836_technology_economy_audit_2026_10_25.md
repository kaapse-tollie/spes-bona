# Gaza’s technological and economic start, 1 January 1836

**Research audit for DP · 25 October 2026 · SB 0.20.2 / installed Victoria 3 1.14.5**

## Executive summary

Gaza is not authored as an industrial economy. SB gives it **one simple maize-farm level**, no commercial ranch or logging camp, and four explicit technology grants: Enclosure, Standing Army, Urbanization and International Trade. The technology package is inherited from Vanilla; SB replaces Vanilla’s one millet farm and 25 authored barracks with the maize farm and a separately authored formation of four regular and fourteen conscript irregular-infantry units. These are source definitions, not observations of employment, mobilised manpower or GDP.[1], [2]

The suspicion of overstatement is strongest concerning **commercial employment and ownership, inherited literacy, and effective control over people and territory**. It is weaker concerning basic farming, military organisation and external exchange. Regional food markets predated Gaza, while dated evidence places Soshangane’s forces controlling passage between Portuguese trading settlements in 1833–34.[3, pp. 33–36], [4, pp. 78–80] Conversely, the kingdom’s later extent and institutions cannot simply be assigned to January 1836.[5]

Retain the four technologies provisionally as broad capabilities; prioritise reviewing what the farm, professions and territorial allocation imply. Enclosure and Urbanization have the weakest literal fit, but removing them without examining their construction and infrastructure dependencies would be an indiscriminate reduction rather than an evidenced correction.

## Introduction

This is a bounded audit of **GZA at campaign opening**, including immediately scheduled effects. It asks whether SB overstates particular capabilities, rather than ranking societies on a single civilisation scale. Cultivation, craft production, organised warfare, trade, literacy and industrial production are separate questions.

Assumptions: the intended baseline is SB with installed CMF and all DLC; the game’s buildings represent aggregates, not individual establishments; “subsistence” is an engine category, not a claim that households neither exchanged goods nor produced surpluses. No gameplay, configuration or test files were changed, and the game was not launched.

## Research approach

Targeted searches on 25 October 2026 combined general web discovery, AfricaBib bibliographic discovery, journal/publisher sites, Crossref metadata, and the USP and University of Zimbabwe repositories. Search concepts included Gaza/Manicusse/Soshangane, 1830–1836, agriculture, regiments, trade and territorial consolidation. English, Portuguese and Spanish material was considered. This was not a systematic review.

The local audit traced country templates, buildings and PMs, population initialisers, military formations and startup effects. Searches found no SB/CMF replacement of the four relevant technology definitions or the tier-6 helper. Vanilla was checked at the actual building/law definitions, not only technology comments. Hail Coloumbia’s Apache history and Gates of the Bosphorus’s Kurdish history also use technology templates alongside distinctive institutions; that is a scripting precedent, not evidence about Gaza.[1], [2]

| Historical source actually consulted | Contribution and limits |

Gengenbach’s Fulcrum book required institutional access. Dhliwayo’s LINCOM publisher description was inspected as a lead, not substituted for the book. No access restriction was bypassed.

### 2. Farming, crafts and commercial scale

Farré describes eighteenth-/nineteenth-century southern Mozambique’s dispersed cultivation, women’s food sales, diversified production and connections to coastal markets. This contradicts an equation of household farming with economic isolation. His discussion of maize replacing other cereals does not establish its exact share in January 1836.[3, pp. 33–37]

Accordingly, **one simple farm is defensible as an aggregate surplus/provisioning abstraction**. Its crop label deserves verification, but maize is not itself evidence of mechanisation. The stronger concern is its social form: [simple farming](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/production_methods/02_agro.txt:1>), lines 1–13, provides capacity for **4,000 labourers and 1,000 farmers**, producing 20 grain at the PM’s workforce scaling. That is neither one household nor proof that 5,000 workers are hired. A manor owns the level; additional ownership employment must be considered separately.[1], [2]

The consulted evidence does not establish such a concentrated landlord enterprise. But deleting the level could also hide marketed household surplus. The choice is between imperfect representations, not between agriculture and its absence. Native [subsistence PMs](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/production_methods/12_subsistence.txt:1>), lines 1–45 and 148–163, already provide grain, fabric, wood, services and some household manufactures. No logging camp or tooling workshop is required merely to acknowledge woodworking or craft skill. Neither these sources nor the script inspection establishes an 1836 Gaza industrial metalworking or firearms-production sector.[2], [3]

### 3. Military organisation, exchange and territorial reach

Santos reproduces Nobre’s account of intercepted messengers and negotiated bead payments in November 1833 and April 1834, followed by contemporary reports of the unsuccessful Portuguese offensive of October 1834. These demonstrate coercive organisation and control over communications before the start date. Her nearby spear-and-shield quotation is specifically from **1856** and must not be misdated to 1834.[4, pp. 78–80]

Standing Army can therefore represent sustained military organisation without implying a salaried European army. The authored irregular type has [1,000 maximum manpower and an empty upkeep modifier](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/combat_unit_types/00_land_combat_unit_types.txt:10>), lines 10–25. Four plus fourteen units are **18,000 nominal capacity if all were full**, not 18,000 verified opening soldiers. The conscription law’s much broader recruitment potential also requires review alongside the population allocation.[1], [2]

Trade should survive any revision. Harries’s extract identifies Gaza participation in slave exports but locates the decisive domestic-slavery expansion decades later; Chewins and Delius reinforce the early importance of Delagoa exchange while cautioning against confident volume estimates.[6], [7] These findings support external connections and retaining Slave Trade as a broad legal abstraction, not an invented slave percentage or plantation workforce.

Mathebula places the defeat of Nxaba late in 1835, settlement at Musapa in 1836 and renewed southern assertion in 1838, while distinguishing direct rule from tribute and raiding. The dates are a working reconstruction, not grounds for a precise new border without further mapping.[5, “Migration of the Gaza into the vicinity of the Hlengwe”] SB’s [Zambezia portion](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/history/states/00_states.txt:3479>) is unincorporated, but its [southern allocation](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/history/states/00_states.txt:3414>) is ordinary owned territory. Ownership still gives access to resources and population beyond a mere tribute relationship. That distinction may matter more economically than the single farm.[1]

## Discussion and implications

The commercial farm and occupation-derived literacy have more concrete unsupported implications than the four broad capabilities. Wealth, chiefly authority, religious expertise and command skill do not by themselves demonstrate reading ability. The baseline helper can turn a generated aristocrat into a 20%-literacy pop and a clergyman into a 50%-literacy pop before territorial adjustment; those are game assumptions, not measured Gaza rates.[2]

Equally, research access and literacy should not be used to erase oral institutions, skilled production or exchange. A credible correction should distinguish the material ability to grow surpluses, the organisation extracting them, and the engine’s chosen employment category. Removing every economic outlet could make the representation less historical even while lowering GDP.

## Limitations and evidence gaps

No source consulted provides a reliable January 1836 census, literacy distribution, farm workforce, output series or battalion strength. Regional evidence does not establish every community’s membership of Gaza. The older literature and recent reassessments share some underlying sources, so agreement is not wholly independent. Mathebula’s boundary argument should inform a mapping review, not silently determine all frontier ownership.

No fresh save was inspected. Final technology ancestry, generated ownership/subsistence/military buildings, employment, literacy weighting, effective trade, turmoil and GDP remain engine questions for DP. Static absence of an authored factory is not proof that every engine-generated building is absent.

| 2 | **Retain provisionally** the single simple farm; **defer** its exact crop and **replace/reduce** its commercial ownership/employment abstraction only after choosing how to preserve household surplus. | Medium confidence in surplus production; low in 5,000 commercial jobs or an enclosed manor estate. | 4 | **Replace** assumptions of uniform direct control with a dated core/tributary assessment before changing population totals. | Medium confidence in the distinction; low in exact borders/headcounts.
