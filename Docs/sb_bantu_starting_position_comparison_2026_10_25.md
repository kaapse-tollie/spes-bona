# Four southern African starts: comparative technology and economy audit

25 October 2026 · SB 0.20.2 / installed Vanilla 1.14.5 / installed CMF · Research and recommendations only

## Executive summary

**Review the economic and educational assumptions before stripping the basic technologies.** Four separately assigned country audits examine Basotho, Swazi, Zulu and Gaza. Their shared starting technologies are the same explicit grants used for these countries in installed Vanilla, and do not amount to an industrial package. The most actionable concerns are profession-based literacy, commercial employment/ownership, and specific institutional advantages. Basotho additionally receives Rationalism and an SB-authored formal British support treaty. [1]–[5]

The distinction to model is a pre-industrial economy with particular skills, institutions and exchange networks. “As advanced as ancient civilisations” is too broad to choose a V3 technology set: it does not specify whether the comparison concerns writing, metallurgy, land tenure, permanent armed service or productive energy. For example, archaeological evidence at Dingane’s capital supports specialised brass working and a substantial settlement without establishing mass literacy or industrial production. [6]

## Scope and approach

The four country briefs are the detailed evidence records. They trace the local history scripts, native helpers, production methods, laws, population initialisation and SB startup effects, then compare them with dated historical evidence. They distinguish primary accounts, archaeology, scholarly synthesis, abstracts and inaccessible material. This comparison checks common mechanics and prioritises decisions; it is not a fifth independent historical study or a systematic review.

All findings concern the authored 1 January 1836 scenario. Authored building levels are not observed employment or output; summed `create_pop.size` inputs are not a verified runtime demographic total. No fresh game was launched, no economic simulation was run and no gameplay source was changed. The detailed reports state their access and evidence limits. [1]–[4]

### Why deleting a technology is not a neutral historical correction

| Technology | Mechanical consequence and interpretive limit |
|---|---|
| Enclosure | Gates farms and ranches. Its private-property narrative is a poor fit for customary tenure, but removal also blocks ways to represent productive agriculture. A tenure correction and an agricultural-capability correction are different decisions. |
| Standing Army | Enables barracks and some other military options; it does not itself grant firearms or Professional Army. SB’s army laws, irregular units and equipment penalties already distinguish these forces. |
| Urbanisation | Adds infrastructure and construction capacity and gates basic construction/urban buildings. Its numerical advantages may deserve calibration, but a deletion changes the entire development path. |
| International Trade | Supports the active Mercantilism law. The old comment about unlocking trade centres is stale: the current building uses construction conditions, not a direct technology gate. Knowledge of exchange does not imply a fleet, trading route or strong market access. |
| Rationalism, BST only | Adds wealth-based education access and schooling capacity, and enables the current Freedom of Conscience law. Modest missionary instruction or toleration does not validate a nationwide educational advantage; changing the technology requires reviewing those dependent laws too. |

All mechanical claims above derive from the installed definitions, rather than their names alone. Exact files and line anchors are in the country briefs. [1]–[5]

### The clearer discrepancies

**Literacy is the strongest common review target.** The inherited baseline is not simply a national 5%: profession increments can produce 50% for clergymen and several technical occupations, 20% for upper strata, with further state conditions. Indigenous religious expertise does not establish alphabetic literacy. The briefs do not establish precise replacement rates; officer and other qualifications must be checked alongside a reduction. [1]–[4]

**Building scale matters more than the presence of cattle or timber.** At native basic PM coefficients, Zulu’s two ranches plus camp, and Swazi’s three agricultural levels, each imply 15,000 potential jobs. Those are capacities, not measured employment. Subsistence already produces several goods, while removing ranches may weaken marketed meat supply. The Zulu camp is a stronger candidate for removal or subsistence replacement than cattle husbandry itself, subject to actual wood supply and construction costs. [2], [3]

**Basotho’s formal British assistance is a separate historical choice.** SB creates a treaty in 1836 with transit, merchant-marine supply and a British power-bloc embassy. The inspected scholarship dates Moshoeshoe’s British treaty to 1843. That makes the early formal package a stronger candidate for revision than ordinary external trade; its function in keeping the landlocked economy connected must still be preserved or deliberately changed. [1], [7]

## Discussion and limitations

The audits support a low-industrial-capacity start, but do not justify replacing specific capabilities with a blanket “no technology” template. Technology ownership, practical knowledge, commercial output, literacy, political integration and access to imported equipment should be assessed separately. The same caution applies in the other direction: evidence of cattle, metalworkers or trading contacts does not validate every native bonus or a particular number of commercial buildings.

Historical population totals, literacy percentages and the correct conversion of households into building levels remain uncertain. Contemporary accounts can contain serious errors and later histories can project mature institutions backwards. The reports retain those qualifications rather than manufacture precise replacement numbers. Source review also cannot establish whether the current starts are economically playable.

## Recommended order of work

1. **Record a fresh start for each tag:** completed technologies, laws, literacy by profession, workforce, building ownership/employment, qualifications, prices and actual market connections. Include the first few months of hiring separately from the start snapshot.
2. **Review the shared literacy profile and learning pace.** Keep starting literacy distinct from later growth, Social Mobility and technology spread. Broader literacy/technology reform remains a design decision, not an implemented result of this audit.
3. **Review country-specific economic assumptions:** the BST British package and Rationalism pairing; SWZ agricultural staffing; ZUL commercial logging and ranch ownership; GZA production, regional exchange and effective control.
4. **Then decide technology changes with their replacement path.** Provisionally retaining basic unlocks is a reversible compatibility choice, not a claim that their European flavour accurately describes each kingdom.
5. **Calibrate military penalties as a combined package.** Irregular units, customary army laws and firearms penalties should not accidentally penalise the same limitation several times. No new penalty or bonus is authorised by these recommendations.
