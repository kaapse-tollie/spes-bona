# Zulu technology and economy at 1 January 1836

Research audit for DP · 25 October 2026 · Spes Bona 0.20.2 / installed Victoria 3 scripts (mod target 1.14.5)

## Executive summary

The suspected inventory is correct: ZUL is authored with two livestock-ranch levels, one logging level, and four technology grants—Enclosure, Standing Army, Urbanization and International Trade. This technology package also exists in vanilla; it is not an SB technological promotion. The strongest concerns are the commercial workforce and ownership abstractions, and the inherited literacy template. Removing every technology would erase documented capabilities while disabling unrelated game systems. [1], [2]

Dingane's kingdom possessed organised regiments, a substantial royal settlement, specialised metalworking and external exchange. These do not establish European enclosure, a permanently salaried army, mass literacy or an industrial economy. [3]–[5] Keep the basic military and settlement capabilities provisionally; scrutinise commercial scale and literacy first. No fresh save was inspected, so employment, realised output, GDP and engine-resolved technology prerequisites remain unverified.

## Scope and research approach

This is a targeted decision brief about the authored 1836 start, not a comprehensive economic history or a universal ranking of civilisations. It assumes SB and Community Mod Framework (CMF) are the relevant mod layers. Local country, population, building, military, technology, production-method, law, startup and institution scripts were traced into vanilla. No relevant CMF override was found. Hail Columbia and Gates of the Bosphorus were checked as scripting comparators, not historical authorities. No game was launched or gameplay file changed.

Historical discovery on 25 October 2026 used web searches for Dingane, amabutho, cattle provisioning, metallurgy and Gardiner; publisher pages, institutional repositories, museum collections and the digitised contemporary narrative supplied separate access routes. Cambridge's scholarly chapter, Gardiner's text and the museum exhibition were opened. Some JSTOR/ScienceDirect material remained inaccessible and is not used as substantive evidence. This is not a systematic review. Contemporary observations are separated from later recollections and nineteenth-century developments.

### What the four technologies mean mechanically

**Enclosure** has no direct modifier in its [definition](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/technology/technologies/10_production.txt:19>). It gates agricultural buildings, including [livestock ranches](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/buildings/02_agro.txt:130>), and the agricultural decree. Its localisation describes consolidation into privately owned fields; granting it does not enact a land-reform law. Thus the name overstates the historical property claim, while its practical purpose is largely to permit managed agriculture. Cattle keeping does not prove enclosure. [2]

**Standing Army** has no direct modifier in its [definition](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/technology/technologies/20_military.txt:3>). It enables [barracks](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/buildings/05_military.txt:16>), [hussars](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/combat_unit_types/00_land_combat_unit_types.txt:674>) and Violent Suppression. It grants neither firearms nor the Professional Army law. The hussar option is an incidental mismatch, not evidence that ZUL starts with cavalry. [2]

**Urbanization** directly adds 2 to the infrastructure-from-population modifier, 40 to its cap, 10 to maximum construction-sector level and 10 maximum weekly construction progress in its [definition](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/technology/technologies/30_society.txt:3>). It also gates [urban centres](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/buildings/06_urban_center.txt:10>) and [construction sectors](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/buildings/13_construction.txt:3>). These are real capacity advantages; a technology grant alone does not construct either building. A royal capital supports settlement organisation, but cannot validate those numerical bonuses. [2]

**International Trade** unlocks [Mercantilism and Free Trade](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/laws/01_trade_policy.txt:5>) and is a prerequisite for [Laissez-Faire](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/laws/01_economic_system.txt:453>); the latter is still obstructed by current laws. The technology file's trade-centre comment is misleading: the current [building definition](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/buildings/11_private_infrastructure.txt:236>) has no technology gate and uses a construction trigger. Trade does not create a port, fleet, route or global commercial network. Its [technology definition](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/technology/technologies/30_society.txt:142>) requires `tech_bureaucracy`, which the four explicit grants omit. Whether the engine recursively grants that prerequisite is unresolved; four authored grants must not be presented as a proven exhaustive in-save list. [2]

### Productive capability versus commercial scale

The basic methods involve no machinery or tool inputs. At full employment and before modifiers, the two ranch levels imply **8,000 labourers and 2,000 farmers**, producing 20 meat and 10 fabric in game units. The logging level implies **4,500 labourers and 500 shopkeepers**, producing 30 wood. These values follow from [simple ranch/open-air stockyards](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/production_methods/02_agro.txt:457>) and [simple forestry](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/production_methods/09_misc_resource.txt:1>). Standard fences and unrefrigerated storage add no effect. The combined **15,000 nominal jobs** are a substantial commercial-sector assumption; the source does not demonstrate their occupancy or profitability. [2]

Vanilla [subsistence farms](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/production_methods/12_subsistence.txt:1>) already model 5,000 peasants per level with grain, fabric, wood and services; home workshops add simple manufactures. The farm method lacks marketed meat. Consequently, removing ranches without a substitute can erase cattle's market representation, whereas removing logging still leaves subsistence wood. Neither building level measures a historical herd or woodland area. [2]

For the historical baseline, Gardiner reports regiment residence for part of the year, organised command and provisioning, cattle payments, and brass acquired through Delagoa intermediaries in exchange for cattle and ivory. He also describes locally made iron weapons, axes and hoes. These support coordinated production and exchange, not a 15,000-person wage sector. [3, pp. 92–98, 105–106] The museum documents Dingane's 1829–1838 capital, enlarged in 1835 to approximately 750 × 500 metres, with cattle pens, regimental accommodation and excavated brass-working remains. [4] Beinart and Dubow identify specialist iron production and royal mobilisation while discussing disagreement over the scale and novelty of Zulu military organisation. They also note limited firearm use under Dingane; absence of modern firearms capacity must not become a claim that guns were unknown. [5, “Knowledge and Power in the Zulu Kingdom”]

### Literacy, qualifications and coercive institutions

Vanilla [ZUL population history](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/history/population/zul - zulu.txt:3>) remains relevant because SB supplies no replacement population-initialisation file. The inherited [literacy template](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/scripted_effects/00_starting_pop_literacy.txt:292>) starts at 5%, adds 45 percentage points for bureaucrats, academics, clergymen and engineers, 15 for upper strata, and 5 for clerks/shopkeepers; unincorporated states halve the result. **This is not an aggregate 5% literacy figure.** In particular, a game “clergyman” representing an indigenous ritual specialist does not establish alphabetic literacy. [2]

Gardiner's very small Port Natal school and court encounters with writing support caution, not a national percentage estimate. [3, pp. 43, 81–82] The profession template therefore has a weaker historical justification than cattle or regiments. However, reducing literacy affects [officer qualifications](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/pop_types/officers.txt:39>); [farmer qualifications](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/pop_types/farmers.txt:74>) depend on wealth and are penalised under Serfdom. Literacy, practical knowledge and qualifications are distinct. [2]

Serfdom, manor ownership and hereditary bureaucracy are coarse representations of hierarchy. They do not establish European estates or written civil administration. Conversely, customary obligations should not be assumed egalitarian. The specific 2,000 Tsonga slaves and Legacy Slavery mapping require a separate evidence check; this review cannot validate that exact legal/population classification. The [wealth seed](</Users/depro/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Victoria 3/game/common/scripted_effects/00_starting_pop_wealth.txt:120>) is similarly a stratified gameplay input, not measured 1836 living standards. [1], [2]

## Implications and dependencies

A comparison with “ancient civilisations” is useful only after choosing dimensions: writing, agrarian property, metallurgy, settlement scale, fiscal administration or military mobilisation. Possessing one capability neither entails the others nor places a society on a single development ladder. The concern is fair where a label imports European institutions; technological deletion is a poor substitute for identifying the particular overstatement.

SB already limits military modernity. [Amabutho System](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/laws/03_sb_amabutho_system.txt:5>) is a Peasant Levies variant with strong conscription and restricted barracks. The initial [firearms modifier](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/static_modifiers/sb_firearms_modifiers.txt:5>) contributes −50% offence/defence and −25% military research speed. [Army reform](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/scripted_triggers/sb_institutions_reform_triggers.txt:2>) requires firearms modernisation; the [firearms journal](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/journal_entries/1-04_sb_firearms_acquisition.txt:110>) advances through treaty or domestic-industry lanes towards 24 months. These restrictions already distinguish regiments from modern professional armies. [1]

Changes also intersect with [court-reach calculations](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/script_values/sb_institutions_values.txt:25>), the [Social Mobility restriction](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/decrees/zz_sb_institutions_social_mobility.txt:2>), kingdom story content and the [£100 weekly tribute proxy](</Users/depro/Documents/Paradox Interactive/Victoria 3/mod/Spes Bona - A Southern Africa Flavour Pack/common/static_modifiers/sb_modifiers.txt:723>). That money is a scenario device, not a historical cash valuation. Stacking economic deletions onto existing military penalties needs an explicit gameplay purpose. [1]

## Recommendations

| Priority / item | Decision, confidence and conditions |
|---|---|
| 1. Literacy | **Replace the generic rationale; defer exact numerical change.** High confidence that profession-based literacy is not historical evidence; low confidence in any precise alternative rate. Separate indigenous expertise from reading/writing and assess officer recruitment before adjusting the seed. |
| 2. Ranches | **Retain provisionally; reassess level/ownership scale.** High confidence in cattle husbandry, low confidence in two commercial levels. Preserve a cattle/meat representation if reducing them. Do not treat manor dividends as evidence of enclosed estates. |
| 3. Logging | **Candidate for removal or subsistence replacement, pending save evidence.** Moderate confidence that one 5,000-job commercial camp is the least evidenced productive block. First assess employment, wood supply, construction affordability and intended opening progression. |
| 4. Enclosure | **Retain mechanically; replace its historical interpretation or defer removal until a coherent agrarian alternative exists.** High confidence in the label mismatch. Deleting the gate alone does not model customary tenure. |
| 5. Standing Army | **Retain with Amabutho.** High confidence in organised regiments, not a permanently salaried European establishment. Keep the firearms/reform distinction and existing irregular formations; no cavalry entitlement is historically established. |
| 6. Urbanization | **Retain provisionally; defer numerical calibration.** High confidence in the royal settlement, low confidence that it warrants the exact infrastructure/construction bonuses. Removing it also affects basic construction access. |
| 7. International Trade | **Retain provisionally for mediated exchange.** Moderate confidence in this abstraction; verify the bureaucracy prerequisite in a fresh save. Removal must account for Mercantilism, trade-centre rules and firearms acquisition rather than silently imposing total isolation. |
| 8. Tenure, slavery and court | **Defer to a focused institutional audit.** Avoid converting all dependency into European serfdom or all customary tenure into unrestricted ownership. Recheck court reach, tribute and story progression alongside any law change. |

## Limitations and evidence gaps

Gardiner is a close contemporary but a missionary outsider; he acknowledges a lost journal and reconstructs material from memory. His national military estimates are explicitly uncertain. [3, pp. 1–2, 90–92] Museum archaeology supports material capabilities independently, although its presentation also incorporates a 1909 recollection. [4] Cambridge supplies a critical synthesis, but its section spans decades: later furnace descriptions and colonial observations are not treated as direct measurements of 1836. [5]

No defensible historical staffing ratio, national literacy rate or commercial output benchmark emerged. Before implementation, DP's fresh 1 January save should establish actual technologies, laws, profession distribution, literacy, building levels/ownership, employment, market conditions and military staffing. This audit establishes the source inputs and historical distinctions, not runtime validation or an economic balance result.
